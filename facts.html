Key Facts & Unit Vocabulary

5+ Specific Historical Facts

Fact 1

In the early 17th century, the Virginia Company popularized the system of indentured servitude to address a massive labor shortage. Port European immigrants signed contracts exchanging four to seven years of labor for passage to the colony, food, shelter, and eventual freedom dues, which were often land or supplies.

Fact 2

The entire Virginia economy shifted significantly after John Rolfe introduced a sweeter strain of West Indian tobacco in 1612. Tobacco became the colonies main cash crop, demanding intensive, year-round labor and large amounts of land, which over time increased the need for workers.

Fact 3

In August 1619, a Dutch warship traded 20 and odd Angolans to Virginian officials at Point Comfort. Initially, many of these individuals were treated similarly to indentured servants, with some successfully gaining freedom and owning land, as the legal framework for lifelong, hereditary slavery did not exist yet

Fact 4

In 1676, a rebellion led by Nathaniel Bacon united poor white frontiersmen, former servants, and enslaved Black laborers against the Virginian elite. Fearing future rebellions from the increasing class of white laborers, wealthy planters shifted from white servants to permanent enslaved Africans.

Fact 5

The expansion of slavery in Virginia was heavily increased by the colony’s use of the triangular trade network, mainly through the brutal Middle Passage. British merchants exchanged manufactured goods in West Africa for captured individuals, transported them across the Atlantic to be sold in chattel slavery, and returned to Europe filled with tobacco leaves
